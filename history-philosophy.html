Timeline Chapters

Period: 1997-2000

Location: London, UK.

Origins:

"The aim is to provide a relaxed and informal forum within which people may examine the big questions. The meetings are led by someone with experience in philosophical research and debate, but the role of the host (or ‘facilitator’) is only to enable the discussion, not to defend any particular position, or preach at the participants."

In 1992, French philosopher Marc Sautet (1947-1998) began gathering with an informal group of friends every Sunday morning at the Café des Phares in Paris. His distinctive, egalitarian approach to discussion quickly drew larger crowds of both active participants and listeners, expanding across Parisian cafés, the French provinces, and internationally to cities like New York and Tokyo.

In 1997 I brought Café Philosophique to London. I was then a doctor of philosophy who had been a publican, among other things, before starting my studies. Recognising both the value of Sautet’s innovation — and carrying not a little dissatisfaction with the closeted nature of academic philosophy — I (founder and editor of Philosophy Now) introduced facilitated philosophical discussions to London. The first meeting took place in The Glassblower in Carnaby Street. The meetings were well attended; press interviews and media interest often meant sessions were oversubscribed.

Period: 1998 - 2000

Location: London, UK / North Island, NZ

Back to the Café:

Not long after Pub Philosophy was established an offer came in from an advertising agency working for Costa Coffee. They wanted to host philosophy at Costa’s flagship café in Soho. This happened at the same time as I was looking for a new venue, as The Glassblower was closing for a few months to refurbish. The deal was done, and Pub Philosophy continued as Café Philo in London for the next two years.

Other side of the World tour:

Following an appearance on BBC’s Broadcasting House, a listener from New Zealand got in touch asking whether Pub Philosophy could be brought to NZ. Fortuitously, with sponsorship from Guinness, I was able to host meetings across New Zealand’s North Island — in pubs, a shopping mall, a school in Auckland, and a hotel bar in Rotorua. Radio and television spots brought renewed interest in public philosophy, though Costa’s support later ended and the café-run venture closed in early 2000.

Pub Philosophy returned to London on the 24th of February 2022 — coincidentally on the same day that Putin invaded Ukraine — as the UK slowly emerged from the Covid pandemic. Recently retired from the NHS after 22 years, I relaunched Pub Philosophy at the Hoxton Cabin in Hackney where it continues to meet weekly every Thursday, 6:15pm–7:45pm in an atmospheric cellar bar. While it did not generate the same pre-internet media attention, the meetings sustained a vibrant interchange of ideas and remain a source of pride.

London Return

Pub Philosophy returned to London on 24 February 2022, as the UK emerged from the Covid pandemic. Recently retired from the NHS, I relaunched the meetings at the Hoxton Cabin in Hackney. We continue weekly on Thursdays, fostering the same lively exchange of ideas that characterised the earlier meetings.

International expansion

The 21st century iteration of Pub Philosophy could not be contained in one country. Leaving the meetings in London to a younger generation of enthusiastic ideasmiths, I moved to Geneva with my new wife Elle who worked there as a human-rights advocate. An English-language Philosophy Pub was established in the Rousseau museum café; meetings began on 20 April 2024 and were immediately oversubscribed (venue licensed for 20 people).

In early 2025 a Wednesday evening meeting was added at nearby Flanagan’s.

Moving on again

In July 2025 Bryn and Elle left Geneva to embark on an adventure that you’ll find details of HERE. A group of regulars — jokingly called ‘the coalition of the willing’ — took over and kept the meetings running in our absence. During our travels, one-off Fireside Philosophy events were held in both the Atlantic and Amazon rainforests, with plans for the Welsh rainforest in Dyfed.

The Philosophy Continues

Every week in London you can find a place to gather and dance with ideas. Pub Philosophy remains for everyone — not just academics but anyone curious enough to ask big questions and listen.
