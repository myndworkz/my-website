# History: Natural Philosophy ## Navigation - Back to Map - ## Left Column - 3D Bubble Dancing with ideas International reach ## Timeline Chapters ### Soho Beginnings **Period:** 1997-2000 **Location:** London, UK. **Origins:** "The aim is to provide a relaxed and informal forum within which people may examine the big questions. The meetings are led by someone with experience in philosophical research and debate, but the role of the host (or ‘facilitator’) is only to enable the discussion, not to defend any particular position, or preach at the participants." In 1992, French philosopher Marc Sautet (1947-1998) began gathering with an informal group of friends every Sunday morning at the Café des Phares in Paris. His distinctive, egalitarian approach to discussion quickly drew larger crowds of both active participants and listeners, expanding across Parisian cafés, the French provinces, and internationally to cities like New York and Tokyo. In 1997 Café Philosophique was brought to London by me, Bryn Williams, a doctor of philosophy who had been a publican, among other things before starting my studies. Recognising both the value of Sautet’s innovation -and carrying not a little dissatisfaction with the closeted nature of academic philosophy – along with founder (and still editor) of Philosophy now magazine I introduced facilitated philosophical discussions to London. The first meeting took place in The Glassblower in Carnaby Street. The meeting, and subsequent ones were well attended thanks for the most part to a fascination with the venture by the British press. I was interviewed by press radio and tv journalists, generating a public curiosity that meant the Pub Philosophy meetings were usually oversubscribed. ### Costa Café and a New Zealand tour **Period:** 1998 - 2000 **Location:** London, UK / North Island, NZ **Back to the Café:** Not long after Pub philosophy was established an offer came in from an advertising agency working for Costa coffee. They wanted to host philosophy at Costa’s flagship café in Soho. This happened at the same time as I was looking for a new venue as the Glassblower was closing for a few months to refurbish. The deal was done, and now Pub Philosophy was back to being Café Philo in London and remained so for the next two years. **Other side of the World tour:** Regrettably, Costa Café Philosophy ended in the early months of 2000. Promises of continued press support from Costa evaporated with a change of agency, and, with the novelty value gone and no more advertising than that I could manage on my own it was decided to call it a day. That was not to be the bitter end for Pub Philosophy. Following an appearance on BBC’s Broadcasting house programme, one listener from New Zealand (!) got in touch to ask whether it was possible to bring Pub Philosophy to NZ. Fortuitously, and with a sponsorship from Guinness, I was able to do just that and hosted pub philosophy meetings in a range of venues and cities in the North Island of New Zealand. Pubs, of course, but also a shopping mall, a school in Auckland and a hotel bar in Rotorua. Spots on the radio and national television brought a renewed interest in public philosophy in the islands, but sadly it was the swan song for Pub Philosophy…for a while. ## London Return Pub Philosophy returned to London on the 24th of February 2022 – coincidentally on the same day that Putin invaded Ukraine – as the UK slowly emerged from the Covid pandemic. Recently retired from the NHS where I’d worked for the intervening 22 years, I relaunched Pub philosophy at the Hoxton Cabin in Hackney where it continues to meet weekly, every Thursday at 6:15 pm until 7:45 in the “atmospheric” cellar bar. Although it didn’t generate the same amount of media interest aa in the pre-internet 90’s, it gathered enough attendees to allow the vibrant interchange of ideas that had defined it in the previous century. It is matter of immense pride to me and everyone involved that every week, if you wish, you can find a place in London to dance with ideas. ## International expansion The 21st century iteration of Pub philosophy also could not be contained in one country. Leaving the meetings in London to a younger generation of enthusiastic ideasmiths, I moved to Geneva with my new wife Elle who worked there as a Human Rights advocate. Before long, the opportunity arose to start a Philosophy Pub /Café in Geneva, and not just anywhere. In the Old City there is a museum dedicated to the life and work of Jean-Jacques Rousseau, built on the site of his birth in 1712. That museum boasts a gorgeous 1920’s themed café. I succeeded in persuading the curators of that museum to host an English language Pub/Café philosophique. Convening on alternative Saturdays between 11:15 and 12:45 the first meeting on the 20th of April 2024 was oversubscribed. The venue is only licensed for 20 people, and that number was quickly exceeded as it remains today - there is almost always a waiting list. In early 2025, I added a Wednesday evening meeting in nearby Flanagan’s (I have always preferred the Pub to a Café) ### Moving on again In July 2025 we left Geneva to embark on an adventure that you’ll find details of HERE. Once again, from among the regular attendees a group of individuals who I named (facetiously) ‘the coalition of the willing’ took over the reins, and insured that the opportunity for inquisition of assumptions continued in their absence. So that brings you up to date, except to mention that on our travels I have also been able to hold one off Fireside Philosophy meetings in both the Atlantic and Amazon rainforests, and hope to soon in the Welsh rainforest in Dyfed.
