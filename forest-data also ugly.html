Agroforestry Opportunity Zones: UNESCO Dyfi Biosphere

Interactive spatial suitability analysis identifying strategic agroforestry interventions that reconnect fragmented ancient native rainforest across the Dyfi catchment.

Project Overview

This project models optimal landscape-scale opportunities for agroforestry, keyline shelterbelts, and silvopastoral schemes across the 80,000-hectare UNESCO Dyfi Biosphere Reserve (Biosffer Dyfi). By analyzing topography, satellite vegetative vigor, hydrology, and ancient broadleaf inventory buffers, this model identifies unconstrained farmland where agroforestry acts as an ecological corridor between disconnected woodland remnants.

Methodology & Analytical Criteria

All source layers were standardized to the British National Grid (EPSG:27700) at a 10-meter raster cell resolution to align with Sentinel-2 L2A ground pixel fidelity.

Measured Variable Constraint Threshold Ecological / Operational Justification
Vegetation Health (NDVI) 0.25 – 0.50 Isolates degraded pasture and open grassland; excludes bare rock/water (<0.25) and existing mature forest canopy (>0.50).
Workable Slope 2° – 15° Targets accessible hillsides while filtering out saturated, low-gradient valley bottoms (<2°) and steep machinery hazards (>15°).
Riparian Buffer 20-meter exclusion Prevents soil erosion, protects water quality along salmonid streams, and respects statutory drainage easements.
Connectivity Buffer 500-meter corridor Restricts targets to fields capable of facilitating bird, mammal, and seed dispersal between ancient woodland blocks.
Ancient Woodland Strict Exclusion Directly excludes protected Ancient Semi-Natural Woodland (ASNW) to prevent disturbing mature native canopy.

Intervention Tiers

  • Commercial Agroforestry (> 5 ha): Large, contiguous field parcels suitable for commercial alley cropping, silvopasture, and machinery access.
  • Silviculture & Micro-Corridors (1 – 5 ha): Niche planting zones ideal for keyline swales, living windbreaks, and community forest gardens.
  • Macro-Hubs (100 ha Clusters): High-density aggregations of viable parcels identifying multi-farm regional cooperation priorities.
